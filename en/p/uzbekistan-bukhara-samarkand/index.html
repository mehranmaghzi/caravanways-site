My Journey Through the Heart of Uzbekistan: Bukhara and Samarkand

Dilnoza's spring journey through Bukhara and Samarkand during Ramadan: the ancient streets of Bukhara, the 18-metre tomb of Prophet Daniel, the turquoise tiles of Shah-i-Zinda and Registan, a simple iftar on the way, and a new friendship between Uzbekistan and Iran.

Dilnoza
March 2022

فارسی Русский Español Oʻzbekcha Türkçe

I have visited several countries and explored different cities, but today I would like to tell you about a journey to two of the most beautiful and historically significant cities in my own country, Uzbekistan: Bukhara and Samarkand.

Why Uzbekistan? Because I believe that before sharing beautiful stories about other countries, I should first share the beauty of my own homeland. I want to tell you about the places that are so close to my heart, introduce you to their fascinating history and, hopefully, inspire you to visit Uzbekistan one day. Of course, I will also share photographs from this unforgettable journey! 😊

The Beginning of Our Journey: Ancient Bukhara

It was March 21, 2022, at the beginning of spring. I decided to take a trip to two of Uzbekistan's oldest and most historic cities, Bukhara and Samarkand, together with sister Gulsora, a woman who has always been like an older sister to me.

This was more than just an ordinary trip. It was an opportunity to explore our ancestors' heritage, visit historical places, reunite with dear friends who lived far away, and make new friendships.

We travelled from Fergana to Tashkent and then flew directly to Bukhara. My dear university classmate Sardor was waiting for us there. Meeting him immediately brought back memories of my university years and all those beautiful, carefree moments. Seeing an old friend after a long time always brings a special kind of happiness.

I introduced Sardor to sister Gulsora, and he kindly accompanied us to our hotel. Since it was Ramadan, he suggested that we have iftar together. So, we began our first evening in Bukhara with a lovely meal and warm conversations with an old friend.

After iftar, we went for a walk through the ancient streets of Bukhara. We visited some of its most famous landmarks, including the magnificent Kalyan Minaret and the historic Ark Fortress.

The Kalyan Minaret in Bukhara at night

When I talk about Bukhara, I always say that it is one of my favourite places in Uzbekistan. I especially love its Old Town. In a way, Bukhara feels like two different worlds: the modern New Town and the ancient Old Town, which has preserved centuries of history.

The narrow streets, traditional houses, historical buildings and peaceful atmosphere of the Old Town make you feel as though you have travelled back in time. They invite you to slow down, forget the noise of everyday life and simply enjoy the beauty of history. This is how I personally feel whenever I visit Bukhara.

Although this was my second visit to the city, I felt as if I were discovering it for the very first time. Every street and every historical monument amazed me all over again.

There is something funny about Bukhara, too. I am not really a fan of cold weather, but somehow, whenever I visit this city, the temperature drops! 😅 This trip was no exception. However, even the cold weather could not stop us from exploring its beautiful streets.

Bukhara was particularly magical at night. The area around Lyabi-Hauz was full of local people and tourists. There were traditional handicrafts, antique souvenirs and beautiful national costumes — the kind of traditional clothing I absolutely adore.

Because the weather was so cold, our friend Sardor even bought warm scarves for sister Gulsora and me. And, as if that were not enough, we ended up wearing his jacket as well! 😅

We spent our first evening at a beautiful traditional restaurant called "Joy", located in the historical part of Bukhara. I absolutely loved its peaceful evening music, traditional decorations and authentic atmosphere. After iftar, we continued exploring the illuminated streets of Bukhara and took lots of photographs.

The following morning, sister Gulsora and I visited Sitorai Mokhi-Khosa, the former summer residence of the last Emir of Bukhara, Alim Khan. I had visited this beautiful place before with my university classmate, but seeing it again with sister Gulsora was another wonderful experience. We admired the historical architecture and bought some traditional jewellery and clothing as souvenirs.

That evening, Sardor invited us to iftar once again. We enjoyed another lovely evening filled with meaningful conversations and memories from our university days. Time passed so quickly that we hardly noticed it. As always, Sardor kindly accompanied us back to our hotel.

From Bukhara to Samarkand: Another Step into History

The next morning, after suhoor, sister Gulsora and I travelled to Samarkand on the famous Afrosiyob high-speed train.

I still remember arriving in Samarkand at around 6 a.m. It was extremely cold, and since we did not know anyone who could meet us there, we had to spend some time at the railway station. We wrapped ourselves in our scarves and even managed to take a little nap while waiting for the day to begin. 🤦‍♀️

Eventually, we set off to discover the historical treasures of Samarkand.

We began our visit by paying our respects at the mausoleum of Islam Karimov, the first President of Uzbekistan. I remember that sister Gulsora and I initially felt a little out of place there. Perhaps, after the warm welcome, familiar faces and friendly atmosphere we had experienced in Bukhara, Samarkand seemed a little colder and less familiar to us at first.

As we were leaving the mausoleum, three tourists took a photograph of us. I also remember one of them asking sister Gulsora something, although I cannot recall exactly what it was. Even such small encounters became part of our travel memories.

Sister Gulsora and me in Samarkand

However, one of the main reasons I had come to Samarkand was still waiting for us: the Mausoleum of Prophet Daniel.

The Mausoleum of Prophet Daniel: A Place Close to My Heart

One of the most fascinating places we visited in Samarkand was the Mausoleum of Khoja Doniyor. This sacred site is surrounded by several interesting local legends.

According to one popular legend, Amir Timur brought the remains of Prophet Daniel from the ancient city of Susa in present-day Iran to Samarkand. It is said that the horse carrying the sacred remains suddenly stopped near the Siyob River and refused to move any further. Believing this to be a divine sign, Amir Timur ordered the remains to be buried at that very spot.

Another fascinating legend concerns the unusually long tomb, which measures approximately 18 metres. According to local stories, Prophet Daniel was an exceptionally tall man, and this is why his tomb is believed to be so long. Another version of the legend claims that the tomb gradually grew longer over time.

What makes this place even more special is that it is respected by Muslims, Christians and Jews. Although historians and religious scholars have different views about the exact identity of the person buried there, the mausoleum remains an important spiritual and historical landmark in Samarkand.

Listening to these legends while standing beside the 18-metre-long tomb made our visit truly unforgettable.

I also had a very personal reason for wanting to visit this place: my son's name is Doniyor (Daniel). Therefore, visiting this mausoleum was something I simply could not miss during my trip to Samarkand.

If you ever visit Samarkand, I sincerely recommend visiting the Mausoleum of Prophet Daniel and learning more about its fascinating history and the legends surrounding it.

When some Iranian tourists heard that we were planning to visit the mausoleum, they offered to join us. We started talking along the way, got to know each other and visited this remarkable place together.

Our next destination was the true heart of Samarkand: the magnificent Registan Square.

Registan and Shah-i-Zinda: The Timeless Beauty of Samarkand

It is almost impossible to describe Registan Square in words. You really have to see it with your own eyes to understand how magnificent and breathtaking it is. Its enormous madrasahs, delicate ornaments, turquoise mosaics and extraordinary architectural details leave every visitor amazed.

Facing the Sher-Dor Madrasah on Registan Square

We took photographs, admired the historical buildings and also visited the Gur-e-Amir Mausoleum, the resting place of Amir Timur.

Afterwards, we temporarily went our separate ways from our new Iranian friends. Gulsora and I headed towards the Shah-i-Zinda complex, while our friends went to have lunch.

We were fasting because of Ramadan. Nevertheless, our excitement about exploring Samarkand's fascinating historical sites was stronger than our tiredness and hunger.

Shah-i-Zinda was probably the place in Samarkand that impressed me the most.

This extraordinary complex contains numerous historical mausoleums built over several centuries. What makes it particularly breathtaking is its beautiful turquoise and blue ceramic decoration. The intricate geometric patterns, narrow pathways and unique architectural designs of each mausoleum make the entire place look like a masterpiece.

It feels as though history and art have come together here. Every step reveals another beautiful detail. Looking at photographs of Shah-i-Zinda is one thing, but walking through its narrow passages and experiencing its atmosphere in person is something completely different.

We met our Iranian friends again at Shah-i-Zinda, and together we headed towards our next destination, the Bibi-Khanym Mosque.

We were so busy talking and enjoying the journey that we hardly noticed how quickly time had passed. Before we knew it, it was almost time for iftar.

Our friends kindly shared some water, pistachios and raisins with us. We broke our fast right there on the way. It was such a simple moment, but one that I will always remember.

Our iftar on the way, by the Bibi-Khanym Mosque

Sister Gulsora and I thanked Allah for His blessings, for allowing us to visit such beautiful places and, most importantly, for bringing kind and wonderful people into our lives. We made a heartfelt prayer together.

Later, we discovered that our friends had captured that very moment in a photograph. It became one of the most touching memories of our entire journey.

We finally arrived at the magnificent Bibi-Khanym Mosque, a historical monument associated with Amir Timur and one of the most remarkable architectural landmarks of Samarkand.

Sunset by the Bibi-Khanym Mosque

The Samarkand sky at sunset: the dome and minaret of Bibi-Khanym

Our amazement at everything we had seen that day was far from over. But another wonderful experience was waiting for us.

On our way, we stopped at a traditional Uzbek restaurant and had dinner with our friends. We ordered manti, delicious Uzbek dumplings that were so soft they practically melted in our mouths! Believe me, I can still remember their taste. 😊

While we were enjoying our meal, sister Gulsora began telling our new friends about our beautiful hometown, Fergana — its wonderful nature, hospitable people and delicious traditional food.

She also invited our new friend Mehran to visit Fergana.

At that moment, we had no idea that this simple invitation would become the beginning of such a beautiful friendship.

From Samarkand to Tashkent: An Unexpected Reunion

Our one-day adventure in Samarkand eventually came to an end. Gulsora and I flew from Samarkand Airport to Tashkent.

By the way, the architecture of Samarkand Airport is quite unique. Its design resembles an open book, which I found particularly beautiful.

Later, we discovered that our Iranian friends had also travelled to Tashkent, but by taxi. They were planning to fly to another country the following day.

We decided to spend one night in Tashkent. At that time, the newly opened Tashkent City Mall was attracting many visitors, so we decided to go there for some shopping and sightseeing.

And guess what? We never expected to meet our Iranian friends there again!

What a wonderful surprise it was! We were all delighted to see each other. We walked around the shopping centre, did some shopping and enjoyed another lovely conversation.

During our meeting, Mehran told us something completely unexpected: he had decided to cancel his plans to fly to another country and wanted to travel to Fergana with us instead!

We were absolutely delighted. We immediately bought him a ticket for the same flight we were taking to Fergana, and the three of us travelled there together.

The Most Precious Gift of My Journey

If someone asked me about one of the best memories of this trip, I would undoubtedly mention meeting Mehran.

Among all the people I have had the pleasure of knowing, Mehran is one of the kindest, most genuine, warm-hearted and trustworthy people. Sometimes life brings unexpected people into your path, and even a short time together is enough to create a friendship that feels as though it has existed for years.

I believe that the greatest treasure of travelling is not just beautiful scenery, historical monuments or photographs. It is the wonderful people you meet along the way and the precious memories you create with them.

This journey brought people from two distant countries, Uzbekistan and Iran, together through the beautiful bonds of friendship.

We have always been happy and grateful to have met Mehran and to have him as a dear friend.

Even after arriving in Fergana, our journey continued. However, I think it would be much better to let Mehran himself, the founder of this website and our dear friend, tell you about our little adventures in Fergana and the wonderful time we spent together.

The Most Important Lesson I Took Away from This Journey

My journey to Bukhara and Samarkand reminded me of something very important: we should first discover, appreciate and take pride in the beauty of our own homeland.

The peaceful ancient streets of Bukhara, the magnificent architecture of Samarkand, the turquoise mosaics of Shah-i-Zinda, the breathtaking beauty of Registan and, most importantly, the kind and genuine people we met along the way have all left unforgettable memories in my heart.

I would love to visit these cities again because some places are simply too special to visit only once. They become part of your heart and keep calling you back.

Dear friends, if you have never visited Uzbekistan, I strongly encourage you to add it to your travel bucket list. Come and explore Bukhara and Samarkand, discover their fascinating history, taste our traditional cuisine and experience the genuine hospitality of the Uzbek people.

I truly believe that this journey will give you the same unforgettable impressions and beautiful memories that it gave me.

Uzbekistan is more than just a country on the map. It is a land of history, culture, hospitality and people who welcome you with open hearts.

Welcome to Uzbekistan, dear travellers! ❤️
